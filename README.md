# PROJECT HOPE
## Hands-On Rocket Propulsion & Engineering

An independent aerospace engineering project focused on rocket propulsion, vehicle design, avionics, telemetry, simulation, experimental testing, manufacturing, and flight validation.

---

## 🚀 Project Overview

Project HOPE is a long-term independent engineering project created to develop practical aerospace engineering experience through the design, analysis, simulation, construction, testing, and eventual flight of a small experimental rocket system.

The project combines multiple areas of aerospace and mechanical engineering, including:

- Rocket propulsion
- Aerodynamics
- Structures
- Avionics
- Telemetry
- Embedded systems
- CAD and manufacturing
- Flight simulation
- Experimental testing
- Data analysis
- Systems engineering

The project is designed around an iterative engineering process:

**Research → Design → Simulate → Build → Test → Analyze → Improve**

---

## 🎯 Long-Term Objective

The long-term objective of Project HOPE is to design, analyze, build, test, and eventually fly a small experimental rocket carrying an independently developed flight computer and telemetry system.

Experimental flight data will be compared against analytical and simulation results to validate engineering models and identify areas for improvement.

A secondary objective is to develop a conceptual two-stage rocket system and analyze its propulsion and flight performance.

## 🧠 Engineering Approach

Project HOPE follows a systems-engineering approach in which requirements are established before design decisions are made and major systems are progressively analyzed, tested, integrated, and validated.

Each phase will produce documented engineering work, including calculations, simulations, CAD models, test procedures, experimental data, design revisions, and conclusions.

Failures and unexpected results will be documented as part of the engineering process rather than omitted.

## ⚠️ Safety

Project HOPE will prioritize safe engineering practices and compliance with applicable laws, regulations, range requirements, and recognized rocketry safety standards.

No propulsion or flight testing will be conducted until the necessary design reviews, safety procedures, supervision, equipment, and launch infrastructure are in place.
